One tick, step by step

A tick is one step of the loop and advances the clock by one sim second; at higher speeds the loop just runs more often. Red steps look at everything to find what changed.

Where it polls

Jobs on fixed intervals

91 background jobs ran on tick counts. Three meant "daily" ran every 24 s, 3,600 times a day: community stats drift, the whole daily-life bundle (diaries, crime careers, gang wars, life comparison), and daily finance. Envy re-fired every few minutes because of it.

Finding what changed by hashing

No system says what it changed. Each tick every agent character is marked dirty and every one of its keys is hashed to find out; big keys every 15 ticks, so a change there is up to 15 s late.

Per-character sweeps every tick

update_agent re-derives desires, deadlines, expectations, body intentions and more for every character every second, whether anything about them changed or not.

The browser polls too

Events and the outliner every 5 s, perception ranges every 45 s, the clock every 3 s, Ollama status. Definitions are fetched by about nine modules on their own. /api/state returns the whole world.

Where we are going: the change dispatcher

A component that changes an entity tells the dispatcher what changed. The dispatcher is the one place that knows who cares: background jobs subscribe by event, the replica takes only the changed keys, and each browser subscribes to the map cells in its viewport plus the things it has open. Nothing is looked at again unless it changed, and nothing static is loaded twice.

Rule 1: say what changed

Writes to an entity go through the dispatcher (changed(kind, id, keys)), not straight into the world dict. That replaces hashing and coarse dirty marks.

Rule 2: only visible changes travel

A client subscribes to map cells, not a radius recomputed per tick. A change outside its cells is never serialised for it. The selected character and open panels are explicit topic subscriptions.

Rule 3: load once

Definitions and the map carry a version. The browser fetches them once and keeps them until the version changes; modules share one copy.

Rule 4: jobs start on events

A job runs because something it cares about changed (or the clock struck), never because N ticks went by. Time passing is explicit and in sim seconds.

Migration steps

  1. In progress. Timers are due at sim timestamps; every interval is written in clock units; calendar jobs run on the clock; one job table (this page is drawn from it).
  2. In progress. Jobs that only checked whether something happened subscribe to events instead (perception, introductions, envy, phone, goodbyes, availability, evictions, archiving).
  3. Next. The change dispatcher: changed(kind, id, keys, cell) at the write sites for position, room, activity, speech, needs; replaces the per-tick dirty marks and the key hashing.
  4. Next. Cell subscriptions for websocket clients; panels subscribe to topics instead of polling; definitions and map versioned and loaded once.
  5. Then. The per-character sweeps in update_agent move behind the events that change their inputs; "time passing" jobs start and stop with their condition.
